# Service split: a database per service and local merchant and menu copies in order-service

At M5 the monolith splits into merchant-, order- and dispatch-service, on ports 8081, 8082 and 8083 with no gateway. One Postgres container hosts one database per service (`merchant_db`, `order_db`, `dispatch_db`), each with its own user, created by an init script. No service can join or write another's tables, and the cross-service foreign keys from ADR-0003 are dropped. merchant-service owns `merchants` and `menu_items`. order-service owns `customers`, `orders`, `order_lines` and `order_events`, plus the WebSocket and every `/merchants/{id}/orders/...` command. dispatch-service owns `couriers`, `offers` and Redis. Each service keeps its own `event_publication` and `processed_events`. To validate and price an order, order-service reads local `merchant_copies` and `menu_item_copies` tables. merchant-service keeps them in sync through two compacted Kafka topics, one per entity: `MerchantChanged` keyed by `merchantId`, and `MenuItemChanged` keyed by `menuItemId`. On an empty start, order-service rebuilds both tables by reading the topics from the beginning. Unknown item ids are rejected with 422, which replaces the foreign key. A synchronous REST call to merchant-service for every order was rejected: with it, nobody could order while merchant-service was down. The cost is that a just-changed price can be a few milliseconds stale, and the price snapshot then records that price. `OrderReady` carries the merchant's lat/lng from order-service's copy, so dispatch needs no merchant data. The split starts with fresh databases seeded by the demo-data script; there is no data migration.
